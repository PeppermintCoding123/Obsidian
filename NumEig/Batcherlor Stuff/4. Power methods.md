baaically having convergence of vectors is difficult to show, so we show convergence in the angle of the vectors.
#### Proof to theorem 4.2
Because $\cos \angle (Q^*x, Q^*y)= \cos \angle (x,y) \forall x,y \in \mathbb{F}^n\backslash \{0\}$: 
$$\cos^2\angle(v_m,e_1) = \cos^2\angle(\hat{v}_m, e_1) = \frac{|\langle \hat{v}_m,\delta_1\rangle|^2}{||\hat{v}_m||^2\; ||\delta_1||^2} = \frac{|\hat{v}_m^1|^2}{||\hat{v}_m||^2}$$
$$\sin^2\angle(v_m,e_1) = 1-\cos^2\angle(v_m,e_1) = \frac{||\hat{v}_m||^2 - |\hat{v}_m^1|^2}{||\hat{v}_m||^2} = \frac{\sum_{i = 2}^n |\hat{v}_m|^2}{||\hat{v}_m||^2}$$
$$\tan^2\angle(v_m,e_1) = \frac{\sin^2\angle(v_m,e_1)}{\cos^2\angle(v_m,e_1)} = \frac{\sum_{i = 2}^n |\hat{v}_m|^2}{|\hat{v}_m^1|^2} = \frac{\sum_{i = 2}^n |\lambda_i|^2|\hat{v}_{m-1}|^2}{|\lambda_1|^2|\hat{v}_{m-1}^1|^2} \leq \frac{|\lambda_i|^2 \sum_{i = 2}^n |\hat{v}_{m-1}|^2}{|\lambda_1|^2|\hat{v}_{m-1}^1|^2} = (\frac{|\lambda_2|^2}{|\lambda_1|^2})^2 \tan^2\angle(v_{m-1}, e_1)$$
since  $\hat{v}_{m}^i = \lambda_i\hat{v}_{m-1}^i \quad \forall m\in\mathbb{N}, i\in(1,...,n)$. $\square$

With  $\cos \angle (x_0, e_0) \neq 0$, meaning the starting vector contains components in the direction of the desired eigenvector $\Longrightarrow$ $\hat{v}_0^1\neq 0$ and $\hat{v}_m^1 = \lambda_1^m\hat{v}_0^1, \; ||\hat{v}_m|| \neq 0 \forall m\in \mathbb{N}_0$
