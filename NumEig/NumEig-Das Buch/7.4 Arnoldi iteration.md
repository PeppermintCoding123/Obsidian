## New Summery:
#### Equation 1.17
Previously we calculated $\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x)$ with Gram-Schmidt and have orthonormal vectors that satisfy 
$\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x) = span(x,Ax ... ,A^{k-1}x) = span(q_1, ... , q_k), \quad k = 1,...,m$  (1.17)
Now sequence $(A^kx)_{k=0}^\infty$ convergence with assumptions on $e_1$ 1. Eigenvector angles against 0?
This is the case $x,Ax,..A^{m-1}x$ numerically linearly dependant.

#### The changes explained: (Why we are allowed to do this instead)
Assume $q_1,...,q_k$ already constructed. => Need $A^kx$ for $\mathcal{K}_{k+1}(A,x)$ 
Since $A^{k-1}x\in \mathcal{K}_{k}(A,x) = span(q_1,..,q_k)$ => $\exists \alpha_1,...,\alpha_k$: $A^{k-1}x=\alpha_1q_1+...+\alpha_kq_k$
=> $A^{k}x=\alpha_1Aq_1+...+\alpha_kAq_k$ $\in \mathcal{K}_{k}(A,x)$ => $A^kx, Aq_k \in \mathcal{K}_{k+1}(A,x)\backslash\mathcal{K}_{k}(A,x)$
=> $\mathcal{K}_{k+1}(A,x) = span(q_1, ... , q_k  ,A^{k}x) = span(q_1, ... , q_k ,Aq_k)$
=> Rather Orthogonalize $Aq_k$ instead of $A^kx$, because numerically more distinkt

#### Now the implementation: Equation 7.18 & 7.19
$h_{ik}:=\langle Aq_k,q_i\rangle$ 
$\tilde{q}_{k+1}=Aq_k-\sum_{i=1}^k\langle q_k,q_i\rangle q_i = Aq_k-\sum_{i=1}^kh_{ik}q_i$ (7.18)
$q_{k+1}=\frac{1}{h_{k+1,k}}\tilde{q}_{k+1}$  (7.19) to normalize, where $h_{k+1,k}=||\tilde{q}_{k+1}||$ 
=> this is how we get the orthonormal basis $q_1,...,q_{k+1}$ of $\mathcal{K}_{k+1}(A,x)$
(Advisable to use GM with reorthogonalization)

#### Further
Let $\mathcal{Q}_m \in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times \; m}$ = isometric matrix with columns $q_1, ... , q_m$
=> Eigenpairs of $H_m := \mathcal{Q}^*_m A \mathcal{Q}_m$ = Ritz pairs of A with respect to $\mathcal{K}_m(A,x)$ 

#### Theorem 7.16
$A\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times \; n}$ ,$m\in\mathbb{N}$, $x\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n}$ with $dim(\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x)) = m$. 
$\mathcal{Q}_m =[q_1,...,q_m] \in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times \; m}$ satisfies Equation 7.17.
=> $H_m=[h_{ij}]:= \mathcal{Q}^*_m A \mathcal{Q}_m$ $\in \mathbb{F}^{\;m \times \; m}$ Hessenberg matrix, 
i.e. $h_{ij}=0 \forall i>j+1,j=1,...,m-1$ (has triangular structure...)
##### Proof
With (7.17): $q_j\in \mathcal{K}_{j}(A,x)$ => $Aq_j\in \mathcal{K}_{j+1}(A,x) = span(q_1,...,q_{j+1})$ .
Because $q_1,...,q_m$ are orthonormal => $h_{ij}=\langle Aq_j,q_i\rangle = 0 \forall i=j+2,...,m$
=> $H_m$ has Hessenberg form

#### Remark 7.17
breakdown if $h_{k+1,k}=0$ for some $k<m$.
We know (7.18) $h_{k+1,k}\neq 0$ if $Aq_k \in \mathcal{K}_{k+1}(A,x)\backslash\mathcal{K}_{k}(A,x)$ 
If $dim(\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x)) = m$ => run for $k<m$ bzw. $m-1$.
For m-th step:
1. $h_{m+1,m}=0$ Algorithmen breaks: 
	$Aq_k= \sum_{i=1}^{k+1}h_{ik}q_i$ for $k=1,...m-1$
	$Aq_m= \sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{im}q_i$
	$Aq_k \in \mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x) = span(q_1,...,q_k)$ for $k=1,...,m$
	=> $\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x)$ is $A$-invariant, therefore all Eigenvalues of $H_m$ are also Eigenvalues of $A$ (obviously wrong...)
2. $h_{m+1,m}\neq0$ next step
	$Aq_k= \sum_{i=1}^{k+1}h_{ik}q_i$ for $k=1,...m-1$
	<=> $A[q_1,...,q_m] = [q_1,...,q_m,q_{m+1}]\begin{bmatrix}h_{11} & \dots & \dots & h_{1n} \\ h_{21} & h_{22} & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \vdots & \ddots & h_{m,m-1} & h_{mm} \\ 0 & \dots & 0 & h_{m+1,m} \end{bmatrix}$ (7.20)
	With $\delta_m$ = $m$th canonical unit vector of length m
	als $m+1$th row is $h_{m+1,m}\delta_m^T$
	=> Arnoldi configuration: $A\mathcal{Q}_m = \mathcal{Q}_mH_m + h_{m+1,m}q_{m+1}\delta_m^T$ (7.21)

#### Remark 7.20
Efficiant computation of Eigenvalues of $H_m$ with QR, since Hessenbergform
For Eigenvalue $\mu$ use inverse iteration with shift $\mu$ for arbitrary starting vector (Section 4.4)

Is Ritz-Pairs a good approximation  of Eigenpairs? $(\lambda, v) \in \mathbb{F}\times  \mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x)$

#### Definition 7.21 Residual
Let $A\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times \; n}$, $(\lambda,v)\in \mathbb{F}\times \mathbb{F}^{\;n}\backslash \{0\}$
residual = $r:= \lambda v - Av$ of $(\lambda, v)$ with respect to $A$.

#### Theorem 7.22 Backward error from residual
Let $A\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times n}$, $(\lambda,v)\in \mathbb{F}\times \mathbb{F}^{\;n}\backslash \{0\}$ with $||v||=1$.
=> $\exists E\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times n}$ : $(A+E)v=\lambda v$ and $||E||\leq ||\lambda v-Av||$.

##### Proof
$E:=(\lambda v - Av)v^*$ and $v^*v=1$
=> $(A+E)v = Av+(\lambda v- Av) v^*v = Av+\lambda v - Av = \lambda v$
and $||E||=||(\lambda v - Av)v^*||\leq ||\lambda v - Av||\;||v^*|| = ||\lambda v - Av||$

=> residual $r=\lambda v-Av$ is small when small backward error, with $(\lambda,v)$ is exact eigenpair of nearby matrix.
residual can be computed cheaply.

#### Theorem 7.23 Residual of Ritz pairs
Let $A\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times n}$, $\mathcal{Q}_m\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n \times m}$, $H_m\in \mathbb{F}^{\;m \times m}$, $q_{m+1}\in \mathbb{F}^{\;n}$, $h_{m+1,m}\in \mathbb{F}$ (as in 7.21)
$(\lambda,y)\in \mathbb{F}\times \mathbb{F}^{\;m}\backslash \{0\}$ eigenpair of $H_m$
=> $(\lambda,v)$ with $v = \mathcal{Q}_m y$ is Ritz pair of A with respect to $\mathcal{K}_{m}(A,x)$ 
with $||\lambda v -Av||=|h_{m+1,m}|\cdot |y_m|$ where $y_m$ = last entry in $y$
(das heißt, dass der Fehler richtig einfach ausgerechnet werden kann)

##### Proof
Let $A\in \mathbb{F} \times(\mathbb{F}^{\;n}\backslash \{0\})$ eigenpair of  $H_m$. 
Theorem 7.3 => $(\lambda, \mathcal{Q} y)$ Ritz pair of $A$ respect to $\mathcal{K}_m(A,m)=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{Q}_m)$
$\lambda v - Av = \lambda \mathcal{Q}_my-A\mathcal{Q}_my$ = (mit 7.21) = $\lambda \mathcal{Q}_m y - (\mathcal{Q}_mH_m+h_{m+1,m}q_{m+1}\delta_m^T)y$ = $\mathcal{Q}_m(\lambda y - H_m y)-h_{m+1,m} q_{m+1}y_m$ = $-h_{m+1,m} q_{m+1}y_m$
while $\delta_m^Ty = y_m$ 
=>$||\lambda v -Av||=|h_{m+1,m}|\cdot |y_m|$
